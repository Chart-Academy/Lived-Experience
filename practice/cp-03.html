Certified Community Practitioner · Module 03 · In-class packet

Team roles: redesign Rosa's intake

For instructors: Print one packet per team. Cut the role cards apart and give one to each person. Any learner can switch to the Team lead role if the case is hard for them. Have 988 and your agency's support contacts posted in the room.
Remember: Rosa is fictional. Never use real client names or details in class.

The case Meet Rosa

Rosa, 37, left an abusive partner three weeks ago with her 12-year-old son, Mateo. They are staying on a friend's couch. Rosa came to a family services center for help with housing and school for Mateo. The center used its standard intake form, in a busy room, with no explanation of what would happen. Halfway through, Rosa got up and left. Later she told the peer support specialist why. The center has asked your team to fix the intake.

“They asked me why I stayed so long. I didn't have an answer that wouldn't make me cry in front of a stranger. So I left.”

Step 1 Role cards · cut apart · 20 min on your own

Intake worker

Did Rosa's intake

Only you know:

  • The intake was in the shared front room. Other clients could hear.
  • You read the questions straight from the form, as you were trained.
  • Rosa jumped when a door slammed, and moved to the chair closest to the exit.
  • She left right after the question “Why didn't you leave sooner?”

You need from another role: A new script for the first five minutes of intake.

Domestic violence advocate

Partner agency

Only you know:

  • Rosa's partner controlled the money and her phone. She is afraid he can find her.
  • The form asks for her current address. She doesn't want it written anywhere.
  • She has told her story to police, a hospital, and a shelter hotline in three weeks.

You need from another role: An intake that doesn't make her tell the whole story again, and protects her location.

Housing navigator

Housing programs

Only you know:

  • Rosa may qualify for housing help for survivors of domestic violence.
  • The application needs some information, but not the details of the abuse.
  • The current form asks for every address she has had in five years.

You need from another role: To know which questions are really needed for housing, and which can wait.

Youth and family worker

School and family support

Only you know:

  • Mateo's teacher says he is “defiant” and “refuses to follow directions.”
  • Mateo has trouble sleeping and gets angry when adults raise their voices.
  • The intake form asks the parent to rate the child's “behavior problems.”

You need from another role: A trauma-informed way to ask about Mateo that doesn't label him.

Peer support specialist

Has lived experience of leaving abuse

Only you know:

  • Rosa told you she almost didn't come in. The waiting room TV was showing a crime show.
  • She said: “I want to decide what I share and when. Nobody has let me decide anything in years.”
  • She said she would come back if she could meet somewhere quieter, with someone she has met before.

You need from another role: The team to build choice into every step.

Team lead

Runs the redesign meeting

Your job:

  • Keep time and make sure every role speaks.
  • Go through the old intake questions one by one.
  • Write the team's rewrites on the worksheet.

Only you know: the center's director has agreed to change the form, the room, and the script, but needs a one-page plan by the end of the day.

Step 2 Old intake, new intake · team huddle · 50 min

Here are eight questions from the center's old intake form. For each one, decide whether to keep, change, move later, or drop it. Write the principle it breaks and your rewrite.

Old questionPrinciple it breaksOur rewrite, or keep / move / drop
“Why didn't you leave sooner?”
“Describe the abuse in detail.”
“Abuser's full name, address, and phone.” (required)
“List every address for the last 5 years.”
“Have you ever been arrested? Explain.”
“Rate your child's behavior problems, 1–10.”
“Do you use drugs or alcohol?”
“Sign here.” (no explanation)
