Certified Survivor Workforce Facilitator · Module 03 · In-class packet

Team roles: Tasha's mobility plan

For instructors: Print one packet per team. Cut the cards apart. Give the Tasha card to a learner who is comfortable role-playing, and the Workforce facilitator card to someone who wants to practice leading the conversation.
Remember: Tasha is fictional. Never use real client names or details in class.

The case Meet Tasha

Tasha Greene, 39, came home three months ago after six years in federal prison. She lives in a women's reentry house and works part-time nights at a warehouse. Her daughter, Jada (15), lives with Tasha's mother. Tasha's supervision officer, her mother, and the reentry program each have ideas about what she should do next. Your team's job is to find out what Tasha wants, and plan with her.

“My parole officer has a plan. My mother has a plan. The program has a plan. Nobody asked me.”

Step 1 Role cards · cut apart · 20 min on your own

Tasha

The participant

Share only if asked an open question or listened to well:

  • Inside, you trained as a peer mentor and earned a certificate in medical billing.
  • You want to work in medical billing, on days, so you can be home when Jada gets out of school.
  • Your big goal: an apartment where Jada can live with you, within a year.
  • You're scared to tell anyone about your credit. Your ex opened cards in your name.

How to play it: If the facilitator tells you what to do, say “Okay,” and shut down.

Workforce facilitator

Leads the goal conversation

Your goals for the conversation (10 minutes):

  • Find out what a good life looks like for Tasha in one and two years.
  • Find her strengths and what's important to her.
  • Use open questions. Don't suggest jobs until she has told you her goals.
  • End by summarizing her goals in her words.

Reentry navigator

Women's reentry program

Only you know:

  • Tasha's supervision requires her to keep a job and stay in the reentry house for three more months.
  • She needs approval from her supervision officer to move or change jobs.
  • Some employers in DC must wait until after a conditional offer to ask about criminal records under DC's fair chance hiring law (confirm details before class).

You need from another role: A plan that keeps her in good standing with supervision.

Training coordinator

Community college workforce program

Only you know:

  • A 12-week medical billing and coding course starts next month, on weekday evenings.
  • Tuition can be covered by a workforce grant if she enrolls through the American Job Center.
  • Graduates often start at entry-level medical office jobs with day schedules.

You need from another role: To know if evening classes fit her life.

Family support worker

Works with Tasha's family

Only you know:

  • Jada wants to live with her mom, but she's nervous.
  • Tasha's mother is tired and has health problems. She wants Tasha to take over soon.
  • A family counselor is available for reunification support, if Tasha and Jada want it.

You need from another role: A timeline for Jada that's realistic for everyone.

Team lead

Keeps time and writes the plan

Your job:

  • Keep time: 10 minutes for the goal conversation, then the team plans.
  • Write the mobility plan on the worksheet, in Tasha's words when you can.
  • After the conversation, ask Tasha: “Does this plan sound like yours?”

Only you know: a free financial coaching program for women offers credit repair help, including disputing accounts opened by an abuser.
