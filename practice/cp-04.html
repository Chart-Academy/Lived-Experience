Certified Community Practitioner · Module 04 · In-class packet

Triads: three clients, three rounds

For instructors: Print one packet per team of six (two triads), or one per triad. Cut the cards apart. Each triad needs all three client cards, the practitioner guide, and the observer checklist. Run round 3 (Mr. Price) last.
Remember: All three clients are fictional. Never use real client names or details in class.

The case Three clients

Three clients are waiting at a neighborhood service center: Mr. Curtis Bell, 71, a veteran who is hard of hearing; Ms. Ana Morales, 44, who is more comfortable in Spanish; and Mr. Darnell Price, 35, who is angry because his benefits were cut. Your team splits into triads: practitioner, client, and observer. Each client card has facts the client shares only if the practitioner listens well. You'll rotate three times, so everyone plays every part.

“Nobody ever asks me what I think. They just hand me a paper and tell me where to sign.”

Step 1 Role cards · cut apart · 20 min on your own

Client 1 · Mr. Curtis Bell, 71

Veteran, hard of hearing

Share only if the practitioner asks an open question or reflects what you said:

  • You don't hear well if someone talks fast or looks down at a paper. Say “What?” a lot.
  • You came about a utility shutoff notice, but you're embarrassed to say you can't read the small print.
  • Your wife died last year. She handled all the bills.
  • You served in the Army and don't know you might qualify for VA help.

How to play it: If the practitioner faces you, slows down, and checks understanding, open up.

Client 2 · Ms. Ana Morales, 44

More comfortable in Spanish

Share only if the practitioner asks an open question or reflects what you said:

  • You understand some English but miss details. You nod even when you don't understand.
  • Your 15-year-old usually interprets for you, but you don't want her to hear about this.
  • You came about food, but the real worry is that your landlord said he'll raise the rent next month.
  • You clean offices at night and can only come in the morning.

How to play it: If the practitioner offers an interpreter and uses teach-back, open up. If they ask, “Do you understand?”, just say “Yes.”

Client 3 · Mr. Darnell Price, 35

Angry about a benefits cut

Share only if the practitioner names your feeling and stays calm:

  • Your SNAP was cut off this month. You start the conversation upset and loud.
  • You mailed the renewal form. You have a photo of it on your phone.
  • You're really scared: your two kids are coming to stay with you this weekend.
  • You were treated badly at another office last week.

How to play it: Calm down only if the practitioner stays calm, names your feeling, and doesn't argue. If they argue or rush, get louder.

Practitioner guide

Whoever plays the practitioner

Your goals each round (8 minutes):

  • Find out what brought the client in, and what they're really worried about.
  • Use OARS: open questions, affirmations, reflections, and a summary.
  • Adapt to the person: language, hearing, reading, or feelings.
  • End with teach-back: “Tell me in your words what we'll do next.”

Observer checklist

Whoever is observing

Make a tally mark each time you hear:

  • An open question, an affirmation, a reflection, a summary
  • Plain language or a check for understanding (teach-back)
  • A calm, de-escalating response
  • A closed question that shut the conversation down

Also: Write down the exact question that opened up the conversation the most.

Team lead

Timekeeper for both triads

Your job:

  • Each round: 8 minutes of interview, then 5 minutes of feedback, observer first.
  • Call “rotate” and make sure the cards move so everyone plays every part.
  • Collect each observer's tallies on the team worksheet.

Only you know: after round 3, you'll lead the team in naming the one question that worked best with each client.
