Certified Community Practitioner · Module 07 · In-class packet

Team roles: fix the notes from Ms. Hayes's visit

For instructors: Print one packet per team. Cut the role cards apart and give one to each person. The Team lead writes the final DAP note.
Remember: Ms. Hayes is fictional. Never use real client names or details in class.

The case Meet Ms. Hayes

Ms. Lorraine Hayes, 59, lives alone and works part time at a school cafeteria. She fell behind on her electric bill and received a shutoff notice. Her pharmacy reported that she hasn't picked up her blood pressure medication in six weeks. A team of workers visited her home on October 6 and followed up the next day. Every worker wrote a note, and every note has problems. Your team's job is to fix them.

“I read what they wrote about me last time. ‘Uncooperative.’ I was scared. Nobody wrote that down.”

Step 1 Role cards · cut apart · 20 min on your own

Outreach worker

Led the home visit

Your note says:

  • “Client was uncooperative and wouldn't let us in at first. Home was filthy. She seems out of it.”

What really happened: She opened the door after about 2 minutes and said, “I wasn't expecting anyone.” There were dishes in the sink and mail piled on the table. She answered every question and said she'd been “feeling dizzy.”

Utility assistance specialist

Energy bill help

Your note says:

  • “Talked to client re: $$. Needs LIHEAP ASAP. Will f/u.”

What really happened: She owes $612. The shutoff date is October 20. You gave her the energy assistance application. She needs her ID and her last two pay stubs. You'll call her on October 9 at 3 p.m., after her shift.

Community health worker

Medication follow-up

Your note says:

  • “Client is noncompliant with meds and probably has dementia. Her daughter is useless and lives in MD and never visits. Client also told me she was in rehab in 2009.”

What really happened: She said the medicine cost went up to $47 and she “had to choose between the light bill and the pills.” Her daughter lives in Maryland and calls weekly. The 2009 treatment has nothing to do with this visit.

Benefits outreach worker

Benefits check

Your note says:

  • “Client might qualify for some stuff. Didn't have papers. Told her to come in.”

What really happened: She may qualify for help paying Medicare costs through DC Medicaid programs. She has her Medicare card but needs her bank statements. She agreed to an appointment on October 14 at 10 a.m.

Peer support specialist

Followed up the next day

Your note says:

  • “Went back 10/7. Me and Ms. H just talked for a while about life. She's sweet. Same thing happened to me with my light bill lol.”

What really happened: You visited 30 minutes on October 7. She said she felt “less alone.” She asked about a senior wellness center near her. You agreed to bring information on October 10.

Team lead

Signs off on the final note

Your job:

  • Keep time and make sure every role speaks.
  • Lead the team through the four proofreading checks for each note.
  • Write the team's final DAP note on the worksheet.

Only you know: Ms. Hayes signed a release for the utility program and the health center only. She asked that her daughter not be contacted.
