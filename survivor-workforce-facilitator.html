Course 2 · 80 hours · survivor-centered

Certified Survivor Workforce Facilitator

This program equips you to support people navigating employment barriers, trauma recovery, and stabilization. Core competencies include communication, documentation, resource referral, and behavior support. It prepares you for the nationally recognized NADSP DSP-I Certification, aligned to SOC 21-1093 (Social and Human Service Assistants).

See the modulesAbout the credential

Credential and labor market

What you're working toward

Primary credentialDSP-I Certification (Direct Support Professional, Level I)
Administered byNational Alliance for Direct Support Professionals (NADSP), through the NADSP E-Badge Academy
OccupationSOC 21-1093 Social and Human Service Assistants
Labor marketRelevant to reentry, social services, and workforce-readiness roles
Projected starting wageAbout $40,000 a year, with a path to DSP-II, DSP-III, and Tier 3 leadership roles
Target jobsSurvivor advocate, workforce navigator, direct support professional, domestic violence program staff
How DSP-I is earned. NADSP awards DSP-I to people who earn 15 E-Badges, including the Code of Ethics Commitment badge, the 50 hours of Accredited Education badge, and at least one Core Competency badge in each of four areas: Crisis Prevention and Intervention, Safety, Person-Centered Practices, and Health and Wellness. Modules 1, 3, 5, 6, and 7 below line up with those badges. Your instructor will explain how you'll be enrolled in the E-Badge Academy and how your course hours count toward the requirement.

Core content · 45 hours

Modules

01The Facilitator Role & NADSP Code of Ethics
Your role, your limits, and the ethics that guide direct support.5 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Describe the duties of a survivor workforce facilitator and a direct support professional.
  2. Explain each tenet of the NADSP Code of Ethics with a workplace example.
  3. Set healthy boundaries when supporting people whose experiences may mirror their own.
  4. Prepare a written reflection for the Code of Ethics Commitment E-Badge.

In-class activity

  1. Match ethics scenarios to the Code of Ethics tenet they test.
  2. Draft your Code of Ethics Commitment reflection.
In your own words
  • “The part of the Code of Ethics that matters most to me is … because …”
02Trauma Recovery & Stabilization
How trauma affects work, and how stability is rebuilt.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Explain how trauma can affect attention, trust, attendance, and job performance.
  2. Apply SAMHSA's six principles of a trauma-informed approach to workforce settings.
  3. Identify the stabilization needs (housing, safety, health, income) that come before job placement.
  4. Use self-care and support strategies to prevent burnout.

In-class activity

  1. Review a fictional participant profile and rank stabilization needs.
  2. Draft your personal wellness plan.
In your own words
  • “A sign that someone needs stabilization before a job search is …”
03Person-Centered Practices & Mobility Planning
Plans built around the person's goals, not the program's.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Lead a person-centered conversation that surfaces a participant's goals, strengths, and preferences.
  2. Write a workforce readiness and mobility plan with short- and long-term steps.
  3. Support choice and self-determination, even when you disagree.
  4. Document evidence of person-centered practice for a Core Competency E-Badge.

In-class activity

  1. Role-play a goal-setting conversation, then write the participant's mobility plan.
In your own words
  • “I kept the plan person-centered by …”
04Communication & Group Facilitation
Leading conversations and groups that feel safe.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Use active listening, open questions, and plain language.
  2. Set group agreements and manage participation in a workforce readiness group.
  3. Respond to disclosure in a group respectfully and safely.
  4. Adapt communication for culture, literacy, and disability.

In-class activity

  1. Co-facilitate a 15-minute practice group segment and receive peer feedback.
In your own words
  • “When the group got off track, I …”
05Crisis Prevention & Behavior Support
Preventing crises, de-escalating, and positive behavior support.7 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Recognize early warning signs of escalation.
  2. Use verbal de-escalation techniques in role-play.
  3. Explain positive behavior support and why it replaces punishment.
  4. Know when and how to escalate to a supervisor, crisis line, or emergency services.

In-class activity

  1. Practice three de-escalation scenarios and complete a debrief form for each.
In your own words
  • “The first sign of escalation I noticed was … and I responded by …”
06Safety & Safety Planning
Personal, workplace, and survivor safety.5 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Help a participant build a safety plan that covers work, commute, and technology.
  2. Identify workplace safety risks and emergency procedures.
  3. Protect participant location and contact information in all records.
  4. Connect participants to DC domestic violence and safety resources.

In-class activity

  1. Complete a safety plan for a fictional participant starting a new job.
In your own words
  • “A safety risk people often overlook at a new job is …”
07Health & Wellness Support
Supporting whole-person health on the path to work.4 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Identify physical and mental health barriers to employment.
  2. Support participants in accessing health care and benefits.
  3. Encourage wellness routines that support job retention.

In-class activity

  1. Build a one-page wellness resource sheet for participants.
In your own words
  • “One wellness habit that helps people keep a job is …”
08Documentation & Resource Referral
Accurate notes and referrals that actually connect.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Write objective, strengths-based progress notes.
  2. Make referrals to DC employment, reentry, housing, and survivor services, with a warm handoff.
  3. Track referrals and follow-ups in a shared log.
  4. Proofread and revise their own writing for clarity and a professional tone.

In-class activity

  1. Rewrite five weak progress notes, then complete two referrals for a fictional participant.
In your own words
  • “A referral works better when …”
