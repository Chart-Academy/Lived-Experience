Certified Community Practitioner

This training builds competencies in case management, resource navigation, service coordination, documentation, communication, ethics, and trauma-informed practice. It prepares you for the Human Services-Board Certified Practitioner (HS-BCP) credential, which aligns directly with SOC 21-1093 (Social and Human Service Assistants) and is recognized nationally by human-service employers.

See the modulesAbout the credential

Credential and labor market

What you're working toward

Primary credentialHuman Services-Board Certified Practitioner (HS-BCP)
Administered byCenter for Credentialing & Education (CCE)
OccupationSOC 21-1093 Social and Human Service Assistants
Labor marketProjected to grow 30%, with about 182 openings each year
Projected starting wageAbout $40,000 a year, the first rung on a ladder toward Tier 2 practitioner and Tier 3 leadership roles
Target jobsCommunity liaison, case management aide, resource navigator, reentry or public-health outreach staff
Credential eligibility. CCE requires HS-BCP applicants to hold an associate degree or higher in human services or a related field, verify 350 hours of post-degree human services work experience (some graduates are exempt), and pass the HS-BCP exam. Your instructor will meet with you in week one to map your personal path to the credential, including the steps you can take now if you don't yet have a degree.

Core content · 45 hours

Modules

01The Human Services Practitioner Role
What the job is, where it happens, and where lived experience fits.4 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Describe the duties of a Social and Human Service Assistant (SOC 21-1093) and three settings where they work.
  2. Map the main DC service systems a client may touch: housing, benefits, health, justice, and employment.
  3. Explain the difference between peer support, practitioner, and clinical roles, and the limits of each.
  4. Name three strengths from their own lived experience that apply to this work.

In-class activity

  1. In teams, each person plays a practitioner from a different agency (housing, benefits, reentry, health, workforce, or team lead).
  2. Together, build a "systems map" for a fictional client, choose her first step, and plan a warm handoff.
In your own words
  • “A strength I bring to this work from my own life is … because …”

02Ethics, Boundaries & Confidentiality
Professional standards that protect clients and you.5 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Apply the National Organization for Human Services (NOHS) Ethical Standards to workplace scenarios.
  2. Explain confidentiality, informed consent, and the limits of confidentiality, including mandated reporting.
  3. Identify boundary risks and dual relationships, especially when serving your own community.
  4. Decide what client information may never be shared outside the agency.

In-class activity

  1. With a partner, sort eight ethics scenarios into OK, not OK, or "it depends," and explain why.
  2. For every "not OK," write what the practitioner should do instead.
In your own words
  • “A boundary I will need to watch in my own community is … and I will handle it by …”
03Trauma-Informed Practice
Healing-centered engagement, safety, and caring for yourself.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Explain SAMHSA's six principles of a trauma-informed approach and give one workplace example of each.
  2. Recognize common trauma responses and respond in ways that avoid re-traumatizing a client.
  3. Rewrite intake questions and agency language so they are trauma-informed.
  4. Build a personal plan to prevent burnout and vicarious trauma.

In-class activity

  1. Review a sample intake form. Mark questions that could cause harm and rewrite them.
  2. Draft your own wellness and boundaries plan.
In your own words
  • “When I notice I'm carrying a client's stress, I will …”
04Communication & Interviewing Skills
Listening, asking, and building trust across cultures.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Use active listening skills: reflecting, summarizing, and open-ended questions.
  2. Apply basic motivational interviewing techniques to support a client's own goals.
  3. Adjust communication for culture, language, literacy, and disability.
  4. De-escalate a tense conversation using calm, respectful language.

In-class activity

  1. Role-play a first meeting in groups of three: practitioner, client, observer. Rotate roles.
  2. Observers score each conversation with a listening checklist.
In your own words
  • “The question that opened up the conversation the most was … because …”
05Case Management & Service Coordination
From intake to closing, one step at a time.8 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Walk through the case management cycle: intake, assessment, planning, linkage, monitoring, and closing.
  2. Complete a strengths-based needs assessment for a fictional client.
  3. Write a service plan with SMART goals the client agrees to.
  4. Coordinate services across two or more agencies and track follow-ups.

In-class activity

  1. Work a full fictional case from intake to a 30-day service plan.
  2. Build a follow-up tracker in Google Sheets for the case.
In your own words
  • “The client's biggest strength in this case was … and I built the plan around it by …”
06Resource Navigation in DC
Knowing where to send people, and getting them there.6 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Locate DC resources for housing, food, benefits, health care, reentry, and employment.
  2. Check eligibility rules and verify that a resource is current before referring.
  3. Make a warm handoff instead of a cold referral.
  4. Help a client overcome common barriers: ID, transportation, documents, and wait lists.

In-class activity

  1. In teams, verify ten resources by phone or website and update a shared resource guide.
  2. Role-play a warm handoff call.
In your own words
  • “A resource that was out of date was … I found the right information by …”
07Documentation & Professional Writing
Case notes that are accurate, objective, and respectful.5 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Write case notes in a standard format (for example, DAP: Data, Assessment, Plan).
  2. Use objective, strengths-based language instead of labels or opinions.
  3. Store and share records in line with privacy rules.
  4. Proofread and revise their own writing for clarity and a professional tone.

In-class activity

  1. Rewrite five weak case notes. Remove opinions, labels, and missing facts.
  2. Write a professional referral email for a fictional client.
In your own words
  • “The note I fixed had a problem with … and I changed it to …”
08HS-BCP Exam Readiness
Review, practice questions, and your credential plan.5 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Describe the HS-BCP exam format and content areas.
  2. Complete a practice exam and identify their two weakest areas.
  3. Use test-taking strategies for scenario-based questions.
  4. Write a personal credential plan with dates for each eligibility step.

In-class activity

  1. Take a timed practice exam, then review answers in small groups.
  2. Complete your credential plan with your instructor.
In your own words
  • “My next three steps toward the HS-BCP are …”
