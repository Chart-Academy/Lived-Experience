Course 1 · Certified Community Practitioner

Human Services-Board Certified Practitioner (HS-BCP)

Primary credentialHuman Services-Board Certified Practitioner (HS-BCP)
Administered byCenter for Credentialing & Education (CCE)
OccupationSOC 21-1093 Social and Human Service Assistants
Labor marketProjected to grow 30%, with about 182 openings each year
Projected starting wageAbout $40,000 a year, the first rung on a ladder toward Tier 2 practitioner and Tier 3 leadership roles
Target jobsCommunity liaison, case management aide, resource navigator, reentry or public-health outreach staff
Credential eligibility. CCE requires HS-BCP applicants to hold an associate degree or higher in human services or a related field, verify 350 hours of post-degree human services work experience (some graduates are exempt), and pass the HS-BCP exam. Your instructor will meet with you in week one to map your personal path to the credential, including the steps you can take now if you don't yet have a degree.

Module 8, HS-BCP Exam Readiness, walks you through practice questions, test strategies, and your personal credential plan.

Course 2 · Certified Survivor Workforce Facilitator

DSP-I Certification (NADSP)

Primary credentialDSP-I Certification (Direct Support Professional, Level I)
Administered byNational Alliance for Direct Support Professionals (NADSP), through the NADSP E-Badge Academy
OccupationSOC 21-1093 Social and Human Service Assistants
Labor marketRelevant to reentry, social services, and workforce-readiness roles
Projected starting wageAbout $40,000 a year, with a path to DSP-II, DSP-III, and Tier 3 leadership roles
Target jobsSurvivor advocate, workforce navigator, direct support professional, domestic violence program staff
How DSP-I is earned. NADSP awards DSP-I to people who earn 15 E-Badges, including the Code of Ethics Commitment badge, the 50 hours of Accredited Education badge, and at least one Core Competency badge in each of four areas: Crisis Prevention and Intervention, Safety, Person-Centered Practices, and Health and Wellness. Modules 1, 3, 5, 6, and 7 below line up with those badges. Your instructor will explain how you'll be enrolled in the E-Badge Academy and how your course hours count toward the requirement.

Getting there

Your path to the credential

  1. Week one: meet with your instructor to map your personal path, based on your education and work experience.
  2. Check the requirements on the official site: CCE for the HS-BCP or NADSP for the DSP-I.
  3. Gather your documents: transcripts, proof of work experience, and your course completion certificate.
  4. Apply, and ask your instructor about help with fees.
  5. Prepare: study your weakest areas for the HS-BCP exam, or earn your E-Badges for the DSP-I.
  6. Earn it, and keep it current with continuing education.
Always check the source. Requirements, fees, and exam details can change. The credentialing organization's own website is the final word.

Where this leads

The professional career ladder

You start here

Tier 1 · Entry

Outreach, peer support, and program assistance.

Tier 2 · Practitioner

Facilitation, navigation, intervention, and case management.

Tier 3 · Leadership

Supervision, management, and training others.

Tier 4 · Enterprise

Consulting, government contracting, and social enterprise ownership.

Both courses are entry points. Your credential is the first step on a ladder, not the last.
