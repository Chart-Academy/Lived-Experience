Many learners will say “I did,” or “nobody.” That's the point. Case management is the job of making sure the client doesn't have to carry the whole system alone. Write down what fell through the cracks. Those are the reasons for follow-up.

Module 05 · Case Management5

The cycle

Six stages of case management

1. Intake

First contact, eligibility, consent, and building trust

2. Assessment

Strengths, needs, goals, and risks, from the client's view

3. Planning

SMART goals and steps, written with the client

4. Linkage

Referrals and warm handoffs to the right agencies

5. Monitoring

Follow-up, checking progress, and adjusting the plan

6. Closing

Goals met, or a planned transition, with a way back
Module 05 · Case Management6
Stress that the cycle repeats. Monitoring often sends you back to assessment when something changes. Closing is planned, not a case going quiet. Ask: “Have you ever had a case closed on you without warning? How did that feel?”

Assessment

Start with strengths

A strengths-based assessment asks what is working, and builds the plan on it.

  • Personal: skills, experience, faith, humor, determination
  • Relationships: family, friends, neighbors, church
  • Community: programs, places, and people the client already trusts
  • Survival: what they've already handled. “How have you managed so far?”

Ask

“What's going well right now, even a little?”

“Who do you count on?”

“What have you gotten through before?”
Module 05 · Case Management7
People with lived experience are often experts at spotting survival strengths that others miss. Make the point: a needs-only assessment makes the client a list of problems. A strengths-based assessment makes them a partner.

MeansExample
SpecificSays exactly what will happenApply for emergency rental assistance
MeasurableYou can tell when it's doneApplication submitted, with a confirmation number
AchievableRealistic with the client's time and resourcesWith help from the case manager on a weekday morning
RelevantMatters to the clientMonique's top worry is losing the apartment
Time-boundHas a dateBy October 30
Module 05 · Case Management8
Write a weak goal on the board: “Monique will get stable housing.” Have the class make it SMART together. Remind them: the goals belong to the client. Use the client's words when you can.

Linkage

Coordinating across agencies

  • Get a release of information for each agency before sharing
  • Agree on one lead so the client isn't juggling five case managers
  • Share only what each agency needs for its part
  • Make warm handoffs, then confirm the client got there

Case conference

When several agencies are involved, a short meeting, with the client present when possible, can fix conflicts like overlapping appointments.
Module 05 · Case Management9
Connect to Module 1 (systems map) and Module 2 (releases of information). Ask: “Which conflict from Denise's case could a case conference have solved?” (The SNAP interview at the same time as her supervision check-in.)

Monitoring

Follow-up is the job

A referral that isn't followed up is just a phone number.

  • Put every action step in a tracker: who, what, by when
  • Check in on the date you promised
  • Mark steps that are stuck and find out why
  • Update the plan when life changes

The tracker

Today your team builds a follow-up tracker in Google Sheets from the course template. Keep notes factual and leave private details out.
Module 05 · Case Management10
Show the tracker template on screen (download it from the module page and open it in Google Sheets: File, Import, Upload). Point out the Status column. Explain that agencies use their own case management systems. The skill, tracking every step to done, is the same.

Closing

Ending well

Close when…How
Goals are metReview progress together. Celebrate it. Give a way to come back.
The client moves to another programWarm transfer with a release, and a final check-in
The client stops comingFollow agency outreach steps first. Send a closing letter that leaves the door open.
Module 05 · Case Management11
Ask: “What would a good ending have looked like in your own experience?” Closing well is part of trauma-informed practice: no surprise endings.

Quick check 10 min

Which stage?

  1. You call the clinic to see if your client made it to her appointment.
  2. You ask a client, “What's going well for you right now?”
  3. You and the client write, “Submit the job training application by Friday.”
  4. You walk the client to the benefits office and introduce her to the worker.
  5. You review the plan together and say goodbye, leaving the door open.

The case · fictional

Meet the Carter family

Monique Carter, 58, is raising her grandchildren, Jaylen (11) and Amari (7), while their mother is in treatment.

  • Works part time as a home health aide
  • Two months behind on rent
  • Has diabetes and has been missing doctor visits
  • Jaylen has an IEP and is struggling at a new school

What Monique says

“I raised my kids. I can raise these two. I just can't do it with five offices calling me at work.”
Module 05 · Case Management14
Point out Monique's strengths in her own words: experience raising children, determination, a job. Her request is about coordination. A plan with one lead and one tracker answers it.

Step 1 · on your own, in role 25 min

Read your card

  • Read the facts only you know
  • What strength of Monique's does your agency see?
  • What does your agency need, and from whom?
  • Write one SMART goal your agency could work on
Module 05 · Case Management16
Check that each draft goal has a date and a way to measure it. Many first drafts will be vague.

Step 2 · team huddle 75 min

Assess, then agree on goals

  1. List Monique's strengths together
  2. Find the conflicts: who needs what from whom?
  3. Put things in order. What has to happen first?
  4. Agree on three to five SMART goals

Watch for

Steps that depend on each other. The kinship paperwork unlocks SNAP, Medicaid, and rental assistance.
Module 05 · Case Management17
This is a long huddle. Call a 5-minute stretch halfway. Make sure teams discover the chain: kinship paperwork, then proof of the children in the home, then rental assistance and benefits.

Step 3 · plan, track, and present 55 min

Build the plan and the tracker

  • Write the 30-day plan on the worksheet: goal, steps, who, by when
  • Open the tracker template in Google Sheets and share it with your team
  • Enter every action step, with a due date and a follow-up date
  • Respect Monique's request: one lead, texts at lunch
Present in 3 minutes: your first goal, your lead, and your tracker.
Module 05 · Case Management18
Teams without a laptop can fill in a printed tracker. Ask each team to show their tracker on screen. Look for a follow-up date on every row.

Debrief · whole class 35 min

Step out of role

  1. What would Monique's month look like without a lead case manager?
  2. Which step unlocked the most other steps?
  3. How did you build the plan around her strengths?
  4. What will you check on day 7, day 14, and day 30?
