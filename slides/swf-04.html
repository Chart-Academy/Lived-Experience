Part 1

Learn

Listening, leading, and keeping the group safe.

Module 04 · Group Facilitation5

One-on-one

Listening that builds trust

  • Open questions: “What was that like for you?”
  • Reflections: “You're proud of how far you've come.”
  • Affirmations: specific and true. “You showed up every week.”
  • Summaries: “So far I've heard… Did I get that right?”

Plain language

Short sentences. Everyday words. No program jargon. Check with teach-back: “Tell me in your words what we'll do next.”
Module 04 · Group Facilitation6
These are the OARS skills from motivational interviewing. Practice one reflection as a whole class.

Group agreements

Build them together

  • What's shared here stays here
  • Speak from your own experience
  • You can pass. Nobody has to share her story.
  • One person speaks at a time
  • Phones on silent; step out if you need to

Why build them together?

Women follow agreements they made. Ask: “What do you need from this group to feel safe?” Write their words.
Module 04 · Group Facilitation7
Be honest about limits: facilitators can't guarantee confidentiality among participants. Say: “I'll keep what you share private, except for safety. I'm asking everyone to do the same.”

Facilitation skills

Managing participation

If…Try…
One woman dominates“Thank you. Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet.” Use rounds or pairs.
Someone is silentPairs first, then share. Never force it. Check in privately after.
The group goes off topic“That's important. Let's put it in the parking lot and come back to it.”
ConflictCalm voice, point back to the agreements, and talk privately at the break.
Module 04 · Group Facilitation8
Each of these challenges is on a role card in Practice.

Disclosure

When a woman shares something painful

  1. Thank her for trusting the group
  2. Don't dig for details in front of everyone
  3. Protect her and the group: gently move on
  4. Check in privately at the break
  5. Safety first: if she's in danger now, follow agency procedures

Try

“Thank you for sharing that. It took courage. I'd like to check in with you at the break. For now, let's take a breath together.”
Module 04 · Group Facilitation9
Practice the script out loud. Disclosure in a group can affect other survivors in the room. The facilitator's job is to keep the whole group safe.

Adapting

Every woman in the room

If a participant…Try…
Speaks another languageA trained interpreter, translated handouts, pairs with a bilingual peer if she agrees
Has limited readingRead handouts aloud, use pictures, never call on someone to read
Has a disabilityAsk what works best. Accessible room, breaks, and materials.
Has a different culture or faithAsk, don't assume. Respect holidays, dress, and family roles.
Module 04 · Group Facilitation10
Ask: “What would have made a group easier for you or someone you know?”

Running a session

A simple session plan

  1. Welcome and check-in round (one word or “pass”)
  2. Agreements reminder
  3. Activity: one topic, one skill
  4. Pairs or small groups to practice
  5. Share back and summarize
  6. Close: one takeaway, and what's next
Module 04 · Group Facilitation11
This is the structure teams will use in Practice and in the capstone.

Quick check 10 min

What would you say?

  1. A participant keeps interrupting others.
  2. A woman starts crying while talking about her ex.
  3. Two participants start arguing about whose turn it is.
  4. Nobody answers your question.
Module 04 · Group Facilitation12
Have learners say their response out loud. Sample answers: redirect warmly to others; thank her, offer tissues, check in at the break; point to the agreements; turn it into a pairs activity.

The case · fictional

The Pathways Circle

A weekly workforce readiness group for women survivors. Today's 15-minute segment:

  • “Talking about gaps in your work history”
  • Two co-facilitators lead
  • Participants play the cards they're dealt
  • Three rounds, rotating leaders

What one participant says

“Every interview, they ask about the gap. What am I supposed to say? That I was locked up? That he wouldn't let me work?”
Module 04 · Group Facilitation14
This quote opens the segment. The topic is real and charged: every participant has a reason for a gap. The co-facilitators' job is to keep it safe and practical.
