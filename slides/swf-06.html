Module 06 · Safety Planning5

Safety planning

Her plan, built on what she knows

  • She is the expert on her own safety. Ask what she already does.
  • A plan covers home, work, the commute, children, and technology
  • It changes as her life changes: a new job, a new address, a court date
  • Leaving, and the months after, can be the most dangerous time

Partner with advocates

Domestic violence advocates are trained in safety planning. Facilitators support the plan and refer for the rest.
Module 06 · Safety Planning6
Know your lane: facilitators support safety planning around work and the program. Detailed safety planning and legal options go to trained advocates. The National Domestic Violence Hotline can help anyone make a plan.

At work

Workplace safety

RiskPlanning ideas

Calls or visits at workScreen calls, a code word with a trusted coworker
Parking lot or bus stopWalk out with someone, change routes and times
Court dates and appointmentsKnow her rights to safe leave (DC law allows paid leave for domestic violence-related needs; confirm details)
Module 06 · Safety Planning7
Confirm before class the current DC rules on paid safe leave under the Accrued Sick and Safe Leave Act, and on workplace protections for survivors. Telling an employer is always her choice.

Technology

Technology safety

  • Location sharing: check phone settings, shared family plans, and apps
  • Tracking devices in a car, bag, or her child's things
  • Accounts: new passwords, two-step sign-in, and check who has access
  • Social media: no check-ins, workplace names, or photos that show where she is

Careful

Suddenly changing everything can alert an abuser. Plan changes with her and an advocate.
Module 06 · Safety Planning8
Technology safety resources for survivors are available through national domestic violence organizations. Encourage learners to use a safer device when a survivor's phone may be monitored.

Privacy

Protecting her location

  • Never write a shelter address or her new address in places others can see
  • Use the program's address on forms when allowed
  • Never confirm she's in your program to anyone who calls
  • Check releases before sharing anything, even with other agencies
  • Be careful with photos, sign-in sheets, and group emails

Ask her

“Who knows where you live and work? Who should never find out?”
Module 06 · Safety Planning9

The case · fictional

Meet Lena

Lena, 35, starts a new job at a hospital laundry next Monday.

  • Left her abusive husband five months ago
  • He lives in the same neighborhood
  • They share custody of their son, 6
  • She takes the bus to work and picks her son up from after-school care

What Lena says

“I finally got a job. I'm not letting him take this from me too.”
