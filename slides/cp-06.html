The wrong door

Start here

Ways to find resources in DC

  • 211 (DC): a phone and online directory of community resources
  • Official agency websites: dc.gov agency pages for current hours, locations, and eligibility
  • Your agency's resource guide: useful, but check the date it was last updated
  • Other practitioners: the people who know which programs actually answer the phone

Rule of thumb

If you haven't checked it in the last 90 days, treat it as unverified.

Verify

Five things to check before you refer

  1. Is it open? Current address, hours, and whether it's walk-in or by appointment
  2. Who qualifies? Age, income, residency, family size, and other rules
  3. What to bring? ID, proof of address, income, Social Security card, and so on
  4. Is there a wait? Wait lists, first-come funding, or intake days
  5. Who to ask for? A name or a direct line, so you can make a warm handoff

Write down the date you checked and where you found it.

Module 06 · Resource Navigation8
These five checks are the columns of the team's verification log. Practice with one resource as a class: look it up live and fill in all five.

Handoffs

Cold referral or warm handoff?

Cold referral

“Here's a number. Give them a call.”

The client has to explain everything again, may not get through, and may give up.

Warm handoff

“I'm calling my contact at the job center right now, with you here, so you don't have to start over.”

With consent, you introduce the client, share what's needed, and confirm the next step.
Module 06 · Resource Navigation9
Model a warm handoff call out loud, with the instructor as the practitioner and a volunteer as the receiving agency. Script: who you are, who the client is (with consent), what they need, what you've already done, what you're asking for, and the next step. Always confirm the client has a release on file.

Barriers

What stops people from getting there

BarrierWays around it
No IDStart ID and birth certificate requests first. Ask which programs can begin without ID. Look for fee help.
TransportationPlan the route with the client. Ask about transit help. Group appointments in one area.
Missing documentsA checklist for each program. Copies kept safely, with consent.
Wait listsGet on the list now. Find a bridge resource while waiting.
No phoneAsk about free phone programs. Agree on another way to reach them.

Keep it current

Building a shared resource guide

  • One row per resource, with the date verified
  • Note who checked it and how (website or phone)
  • Mark anything older than 90 days for re-checking
  • Add tips: “Ask for intake, not the front desk.” “Arrive before 9.”

Quick check 10 min

What would you check?

  1. A flyer from two years ago says a food pantry is open Saturdays.
  2. A client says, “My friend said the job center does walk-ins.”
  3. Your agency's guide lists a phone number with no name.
  4. A program's website says “funding available” with no date.
Module 06 · Resource Navigation12
For each one, have learners name which of the five checks they'd do first. There's no single right answer. The habit of checking is the point.

The case · fictional

Meet Kevin

Kevin Brooks, 26, came home from prison two weeks ago.

  • Sleeping on a friend's couch, which can't last
  • No ID, no income, phone works only on Wi-Fi
  • Wants to work and to see his 4-year-old daughter
  • Your team has an old resource guide. Is it still right?

What Kevin says

“I've got a list of 20 places people told me to go. I don't have bus fare for 20 places. Tell me which three.”
Module 06 · Resource Navigation14
Kevin's request is the task: put the resources in order, and only send him where it will work. Remind teams that the listings on their cards come from an old, made-up guide. The point is to find the current, official information.

Step 1 · verify, in role 20 min

Check your listing

  1. Read the old listing on your card
  2. Find the official website. Is the listing still right?
  3. If you can, call. Ask the five questions.
  4. Write down what changed, the date, and your source

On the phone

“Hi, I'm a student in a human services training program. I'm updating our resource guide. Can I ask a few questions about your services?”
Module 06 · Resource Navigation16
Walk the room. If calls go to voicemail, that's real-world data too: note it. Remind learners not to give any real client information on calls. They're verifying general information only.

Step 2 · team huddle 50 min

Report back and log it

  • Each role reports: what was wrong, and what's right now
  • Fill in the verification log together
  • Add your rows to the class resource guide
  • Notice: how many doors depend on Kevin's ID?
Module 06 · Resource Navigation17
Ask each team which listing was most out of date. Most will find at least one. Make sure the ID chain comes up.

Step 3 · plan and present 40 min

Kevin's first three stops

  • Kevin's first three stops, in order, and why
  • One barrier and how you'll get around it
  • Act out one warm handoff call: two people, 1 minute
Present in 3 minutes, including the call.
Module 06 · Resource Navigation18
Ask teams whether they can group stops in one part of town or handle some online or by phone. Kevin asked for three, not twenty.

Debrief · whole class 30 min

Step out of role

  1. What was out of date? What would it have cost Kevin?
  2. Which resource was easiest to verify? Hardest?
  3. What did it feel like to make the call?
  4. Have you ever been given a list like Kevin's? What would have helped?
