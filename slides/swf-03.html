Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning5

Person-centered thinking

What's important to her, and for her

Important TO her

What makes her life meaningful: her children, faith, independence, her own money, being respected

Important FOR her

What keeps her safe and healthy: safety, health care, stable housing

A good plan balances both. A plan that only covers what's important for her won't last.

Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning6
This distinction is central to person-centered practice in direct support. Ask: “What's important to you that a program never asked about?”

The conversation

Questions that open up her goals

Instead of…Ask…
“What job do you want?”“What would a good life look like for you in two years?”
“What are your barriers?”“What's gotten in the way before? What helped?”
“Do you have skills?”“What are you proud of? What do people come to you for?”
“You should apply here.”“What do you think of this option? What would make it work for you?”

Financial abuse often leaves women with ruined credit, debts in their name, and no savings. Point out free financial coaching and credit-building resources available in DC (confirm before class).

Self-determination

When you disagree

  • Share information and options, clearly and honestly
  • Ask what she thinks, and why
  • Help her think through what could happen
  • Respect her decision, and stay available

The exception

If someone is in immediate danger, follow your agency's safety procedures. Otherwise, it's her life and her choice.
Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning10
For women who survived control, having someone respect her choice can be healing. Ask: “What's the difference between advice and control?”

Your E-Badge

Evidence of person-centered practice

  • Today's mobility plan can be a work sample for your DSP-I portfolio
  • Show her voice: her goals in her words
  • Show her strengths, not just barriers
  • Show her choices and how the plan respects them
  • Never include real names or details. Use the fictional case.

The case · fictional

Meet Tasha

Tasha Greene, 39, came home three months ago after six years in federal prison.

  • Lives in a women's reentry house
  • Part-time job at a warehouse, nights
  • Her daughter, 15, lives with Tasha's mother
  • Everyone has an opinion about what she should do next

What Tasha says

“My parole officer has a plan. My mother has a plan. The program has a plan. Nobody asked me.”
Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning14
Tasha's quote is today's lesson. The team's job is to find her plan, not give her another one.

Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning15
Give the Tasha card to a learner who is comfortable role-playing. Tell her to answer closed questions briefly and open up only when the facilitator really listens.

Step 1 · read your card 20 min

Get ready

  • Tasha: read your card. Open up only to open questions.
  • Facilitator: plan your first three open questions
  • Everyone else: read what you know. Listen for what fits her goals.
Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning16
Check that each facilitator has open questions ready.

Step 2 · goal conversation and huddle 50 min

Listen first, then plan

  1. Goal conversation: 10 minutes. The team listens and takes notes.
  2. Huddle: each role adds what it knows
  3. Find what fits her goals, and what's in the way
  4. Start the plan, one area at a time

Listen for

Medical billing. Days. Jada. An apartment. Credit. Did your facilitator find all five?
Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning17
After the conversation, ask each team how many of Tasha's hidden goals came out. Let the facilitator try again for 3 minutes if key goals are missing.

Step 3 · plan and present 40 min

Tasha's mobility plan

  • Her goals in her words
  • Steps for 30 days, 6 months, and 1 year
  • How the plan fits supervision, Jada, and her credit
  • Ask Tasha: does this sound like yours?
Present in 2 minutes. Tasha says whether it's her plan.
Module 03 · Person-Centered Planning18
Tasha's verdict is the best test of person-centered practice.

Debrief · whole class 30 min

Step out of role

  1. Tasha: when did you start opening up?
  2. Facilitators: what was hardest about not giving advice?
  3. What was important TO Tasha? What was important FOR her?
  4. Have you ever had a plan made for you? How was today different?
