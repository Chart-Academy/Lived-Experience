Opening circle 15 min

What would your file say?

Imagine a caseworker wrote a note about you on your hardest day. What would you want it to say? What would you never want it to say?

Share as much or as little as you want. You can always pass.

Module 07 · Documentation4
Let learners think quietly, then share only if they want to. Write the “want it to say” list on the board: what I was dealing with, that I showed up, what I'm good at, the plan. The “never” list will be full of labels: difficult, non-compliant, manipulative. That's today's lesson.

The test: write every note as if the client will read it, because they might. Write it the same day, while it's fresh.

The format

DAP notes

What goes hereExample
DataFacts: what you saw, heard, and did. Quotes in quotation marks.Met with client at her home, 45 min. Client said, “I'm worried about the rent.” Showed a landlord notice dated 10/1.
AssessmentYour professional view, based on the dataClient is at risk of eviction. She is organized and kept all her paperwork.
PlanNext steps: who, what, by whenWorker will submit rental assistance referral by 10/8. Client will bring lease to the office 10/9 at 10 a.m.
Module 07 · Documentation7
Other formats exist (SOAP, BIRP, narrative). Agencies choose their own. DAP is common and easy to learn. Point out that the Assessment is still based on the facts, never on feelings about the client.

Language

Facts, not labels

Instead of…Write…
“Client was hostile.”“Client raised his voice and said, ‘You people never help.’”
“Non-compliant with plan.”“Client did not attend the 10/2 or 10/9 appointments. Said bus fare was the barrier.”
“House was a mess.”“Dishes and clothing on the floor of the kitchen and living room.”
“Ex-con” / “addict”“Returning citizen” / “person in recovery” (only if relevant and allowed)
“Seems depressed.”“Client said she has ‘no energy’ and is sleeping 12 hours a day.” (Refer; don't diagnose.)
Module 07 · Documentation8
This connects to Module 1 (know your lane: don't diagnose) and Module 3 (trauma-informed language). Ask: “Which of these labels has been used about you or someone you know?”

Privacy

Minimum necessary

  • Write only what's needed for the service. Leave out gossip and private details that don't matter.
  • HIPAA protects health information held by health plans, health care providers, and their partners.
  • 42 CFR Part 2 gives extra protection to substance use disorder treatment records.
  • Share only with a release, only what the release covers.

Store it safely

Lock paper files. Never leave screens open. Don't text or email client details from personal accounts. Follow agency policy.
Module 07 · Documentation9
Keep this practical. Learners don't need to be lawyers. They need habits: minimum necessary, a release before sharing, and secure storage. Your agency's privacy officer is the expert. Tell learners to ask which rules apply at their job.

Professional writing

A referral email

  1. Subject: clear, with no client name. “Referral: job training, consent on file”
  2. Who you are and your agency
  3. What the client needs, minimum necessary
  4. What you've done already
  5. What you're asking, and by when
  6. How to reach you

Before you send

Is it the right person? Is there a release? Did you use a secure method? Did you read it out loud once?
Module 07 · Documentation10
Many agencies send client details only through secure systems, not regular email. Teach the structure, and tell learners to follow their agency's rules for how to send.

Proofread

Four checks before you sign

  1. Accurate: dates, times, names, and quotes are right
  2. Objective: no labels or opinions written as facts
  3. Complete: D, A, and P are all there, and the note is signed and dated
  4. Professional: no slang, texting shortcuts, or unclear abbreviations
Never change or delete a signed note. Add a dated correction, following agency policy.

Quick check 10 min

Fact or label?

  1. “Client was very rude.”
  2. “Client arrived 25 minutes late and said the bus didn't come.”
  3. “Client is lazy about job searching.”
  4. “Client said, ‘I applied to three jobs this week.’”
  5. “Client is probably using again.”
Module 07 · Documentation12
Labels: 1, 3, 5. Facts: 2, 4. Have learners rewrite 1, 3, and 5 out loud. For 5, there are no facts in the note at all. What would you need to observe or hear, and is it your role to write it?

The case · fictional

Meet Ms. Hayes

Ms. Lorraine Hayes, 59, had a home visit after she fell behind on her utility bill and stopped picking up her medications.

  • Five workers were at the visit or followed up
  • Each one wrote a note
  • Every note has problems
  • Your team writes the one note that gets it right

What Ms. Hayes says

“I read what they wrote about me last time. ‘Uncooperative.’ I was scared. Nobody wrote that down.”

Learners may enjoy finding the problems. Keep the focus on fixing: every problem should end with a rewrite.

Step 1 · on your own, in role 20 min

Find the problems

  • Read your weak note
  • Read what really happened
  • List every problem: labels, opinions, missing facts, private details, slang, missing plan
  • Rewrite your note on your own first
Module 07 · Documentation16
Most learners find the obvious labels quickly. Push them to find the missing information too: dates, amounts, next steps.

Step 2 · team huddle 40 min

Check and sort

  1. Each role reads its weak note out loud
  2. Run the four checks: accurate, objective, complete, professional
  3. Sort every fact into D, A, or P
  4. Decide what to leave out

Ask

Is this needed for the service? Does the release cover it? Would I be okay with Ms. Hayes reading it?
Module 07 · Documentation17
Check that teams leave out the 2009 treatment history (not relevant, and protected) and don't contact the daughter.

Step 3 · plan and present 30 min

One strong note and an email

  • Write one DAP note for the October 6 visit
  • Write a referral email to the utility program: subject line and body
  • Name what you left out, and why
Present in 2 minutes. Read your Assessment out loud.
Module 07 · Documentation18
Have teams read their Assessment line. Ask the class: would Ms. Hayes feel this is fair?

Debrief · whole class 25 min

Step out of role

  1. Which weak note would hurt Ms. Hayes most if she read it?
  2. What did you leave out, and why?
  3. How did the facts change the story?
  4. How will you write about clients from your own community?
