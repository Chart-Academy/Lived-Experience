Women's lives

Trauma many women carry

  • Domestic violence: physical, emotional, sexual, and financial abuse by a partner
  • Sexual violence at any age
  • Trafficking: forced labor or commercial sex through force, fraud, or coercion
  • Incarceration: separation from children, and trauma before and during
  • Homelessness and unsafe housing
  • Community and historical trauma, including racism
Many women have lived through more than one. Assume any woman could have a trauma history, without asking her to prove it.
Module 02 · Trauma & Stabilization6
Keep this factual and brief. Women in the room may have lived through these. Name that the course takes a strengths-based view: survivors are experts in survival.

SAMHSA's six principles

A trauma-informed approach

Safety

Physical and emotional safety, including her location

Trustworthiness and transparency

No surprises. Explain every step.

Peer support

Women with lived experience as guides

Collaboration and mutuality

Power shared, not held over her

Empowerment, voice, and choice

She decides. Build on her strengths.

Cultural, historical, and gender issues

Gender-responsive and culturally grounded
Module 02 · Trauma & Stabilization8
From SAMHSA's Guidance for a Trauma-Informed Approach (2014). For women who survived control, choice is healing in itself. Ask: “Which principle did the woman who walked beside you live out?”

Gender-responsive

What it means for women

  • Relationships matter: women often heal and grow through connection
  • Children are central: custody, child care, and school shape every decision
  • Safety is ongoing: leaving can be the most dangerous time
  • Economic abuse is common: ruined credit, no savings, and no work history
  • Women-only spaces can feel safer for women who were harmed by men
Module 02 · Trauma & Stabilization9
Gender-responsive programs design services around women's lives and pathways. Stress that the skills apply to anyone you support, and that every woman is different. Ask, don't assume.

Stabilization

Stability before a job search

  1. Safety: is she safe today, tonight, and on the way to work?
  2. Shelter: a stable place to sleep
  3. Children: safe care and school
  4. Health: urgent physical and mental health needs
  5. Income: benefits or emergency funds to bridge the gap
  6. Documents: ID, Social Security card, birth certificates

Her order, not ours

The order is a guide. Ask her what feels most urgent. A job can be part of stabilization when she's ready.
Module 02 · Trauma & Stabilization10
Many women want a job immediately, and income can mean safety. The skill is helping her see what has to be in place for a job to last, without taking over the decision.

Taking care of you

Burnout and vicarious trauma

  • Vicarious trauma: taking on effects from hearing women's stories again and again
  • Triggers: a story close to your own can bring old feelings back
  • Burnout: exhaustion from workload and stress over time

Your plan

Supervision. Someone outside work. A way to leave work at work. Your own support when you need it. That's strength, not weakness.

Learners will build a wellness plan in Apply. Remind them of 988 and any employee assistance program at their future job.

Quick check 10 min

What might be going on?

  1. A participant misses every Tuesday session.
  2. A woman freezes when a male staff member raises his voice in the hallway.
  3. A participant refuses to give her home address on a form.
  4. A woman wants to start work tomorrow, but she's sleeping in her car with her kids.
Module 02 · Trauma & Stabilization12
Possible answers: court dates or supervision check-ins; a trauma response; safety, she may be hiding from an abuser; stabilization needs come first, but respect her urgency and look for a bridge like emergency shelter and income.

The case · fictional

Meet Marisol

Marisol, 28, left an abusive partner six weeks ago with her two children, ages 3 and 8.

  • Staying in a domestic violence shelter, which has a 90-day limit
  • Her partner controlled all the money
  • Worked as a home health aide before he made her quit
  • Wants a job right now

What Marisol says

“I need my own money. I'm never depending on a man again. Just help me find a job.”
Module 02 · Trauma & Stabilization14
Marisol's urgency is valid: income is safety for her. The team's job is to honor that and make sure the job can last.

Step 2 · team huddle 50 min

Rank the needs together

  1. Each role shares what it knows
  2. List every need on the worksheet
  3. Mark each one: now, 30 days, or later
  4. Find what unlocks other steps

Remember

Marisol wants a job now. How can the plan honor that and still keep her and her children safe?

Teams should find the chains: documents unlock benefits; child care unlocks work; safety planning is needed for the commute and the address question.

Marisol's first week
