The role

What a survivor workforce facilitator does

  • Welcomes women into workforce programs and builds trust
  • Helps each woman set her own goals for safety, stability, and work
  • Leads workforce readiness groups and workshops
  • Connects women to housing, safety, health, child care, and benefits
  • Supports women through job search, training, and the first months on the job
  • Keeps accurate, private records and works with a team

Settings: domestic violence and anti-trafficking programs, women's reentry programs, shelters, workforce centers, and community nonprofits.

Module 01 · Role & Code of Ethics6
Connect to the opening list. Point out that direct support professionals (DSPs) work in many settings. The DSP-I credential recognizes the skills and ethics of direct support work. In this course, those skills are applied to supporting women survivors on the path to work.

Ethics in practice

What the tenets look like at work

TenetIn a women's workforce program
Self-determinationShe chooses whether to take a job offer, even if you think she should
ConfidentialityYou never tell an employer she is a survivor without her written consent
Justice and fairnessEvery woman gets the same chance at the job leads, not just the ones staff like
RelationshipsYou support her friendships and family ties, without becoming her friend yourself
AdvocacyYou speak up when an employer's policy unfairly shuts women out

Boundaries

When her story sounds like yours

  • Notice it: a racing heart, wanting to rescue her, or thinking about her case at night
  • Share wisely: brief, only if it helps her, and then bring it back to her
  • No dual relationships: not her friend, landlord, employer, or social media follower
  • Use supervision: talk it through with your supervisor, not with her

Ask yourself

“Who is this for: her, or me?”

“Would I do this for every woman in the program?”
Module 01 · Role & Code of Ethics10
This is the heart of a lived experience course. Normalize it: being moved by a woman's story means you care. Boundaries are what let you keep caring for years without burning out.

Your credential

The path to DSP-I

  • 15 E-Badges through the NADSP E-Badge Academy
  • First: the Code of Ethics Commitment E-Badge (today)
  • 50 hours of accredited education
  • Core competencies: Crisis Prevention and Intervention, Safety, Person-Centered Practices, Health and Wellness
  • A portfolio of four work samples

In this course

Modules 3, 5, 6, and 7 line up with the core competency badges. Several assignments are built to be work samples.

Which tenet?

  1. A woman decides to turn down a job offer because the shift ends after dark. You respect it.
  2. An employer calls asking why a participant is in your program. You don't say.
  3. You make sure every woman hears about the new job fair, not just the ones you know best.
  4. You help a woman reconnect with her sister, who has been a good support.
  5. You tell your manager a hiring partner won't consider any woman with a record.

Answers: 1) Self-determination. 2) Confidentiality. 3) Justice, fairness, and equity. 4) Relationships. 5) Advocacy. More than one tenet can fit. Accept good reasoning.

The case · fictional

Meet Angela

Angela, 31, joined Women Rising, a workforce program for women survivors.

  • Left an abusive relationship two years ago
  • Lived in a shelter for 18 months, now in transitional housing
  • Worked in restaurants for 10 years
  • Wants a full-time job with health insurance

What Angela says

“For years someone else made every decision for me. I need people who will let me make my own, even the wrong ones.”
Module 01 · Role & Code of Ethics14
Angela's words point straight to self-determination. Keep coming back to them during the case: whose decision is it?

Step 2 · team huddle 40 min

Decide together

  1. Each role shares its question
  2. Name the tenet
  3. List the options and what could happen
  4. Decide, and write it on the ethics table

Keep asking

“Whose decision is this?” and “Would we do this for every woman in the program?”
