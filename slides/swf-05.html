Part 1

Learn

Prevent, calm, support, and know when to call for help.

Module 05 · Crisis Prevention5

Why it happens

Behavior is communication

Behavior that looks like “acting out” is often a woman trying to meet a need or protect herself:

  • Feeling unsafe or threatened
  • Feeling controlled, disrespected, or ignored
  • Exhaustion, hunger, pain, or fear about her children
  • A trigger that brings back past trauma

Ask

Not “What's wrong with her?” but “What does she need right now?”
Module 05 · Crisis Prevention6
Connect to Module 2. For survivors, a supervisor's raised voice or a locked door can feel like danger.

Early warning signs

See it coming

StageWhat you might seeWhat helps
CalmEngaged, relaxedBuild trust and routines
TriggeredTense face, quiet, pacing, checking her phoneCheck in privately: “You seem stressed. What's going on?”
EscalatingLouder voice, arguing, swearingDe-escalate: calm voice, space, choices
CrisisThreats, throwing things, running outSafety first. Get help.
RecoveryTired, embarrassed, quietSupport, no shaming. Talk later.
Module 05 · Crisis Prevention7
The earlier you notice, the easier it is. Most crises can be prevented at the triggered stage.

De-escalation

Calm it down

  1. Check safety: your exit, her exit, and others nearby
  2. Give space: at least two arm lengths. Stand at an angle.
  3. Lower your voice and slow down
  4. Name the feeling: “You're really upset. I get it.”
  5. Offer choices: “Do you want to sit here or step outside with me?”
  6. Don't argue, threaten, or touch

Never say

“Calm down.”
“You need to…”
“If you don't stop, I'll…”
Module 05 · Crisis Prevention8
Practice the phrases out loud as a class. Ask learners to add phrases that worked for them.

Positive behavior support

Support, not punishment

Punishment approachPositive behavior support
“She's kicked out of the program for yelling.”“What triggered it? How can we prevent it?”
Focuses on stopping the behaviorFocuses on the need behind it
Rules enforced the same way, no matter whatChanges the environment and teaches new skills
Often re-traumatizesBuilds safety and trust
Module 05 · Crisis Prevention9
Positive behavior support doesn't mean no limits. It means limits with understanding, and plans to prevent the next crisis.

When to get help

Know when it's beyond you

SituationCall

She talks about ending her lifeStay with her. Call or text 988. Get your supervisor.
An abuser shows upAgency safety plan, security, and 911 if needed
You're unsureYour supervisor, right away
Module 05 · Crisis Prevention10
Make sure learners know never to stay in an unsafe situation to finish a conversation. Confirm any DC crisis lines before class.

After a crisis

Recovery and debrief

  • Check on her: safety, needs, and how she's feeling
  • No shaming. Talk about it when she's calm.
  • Plan together: what helps next time?
  • Check on yourself and your coworkers
  • Document facts, not labels, the same day

The case · fictional

Meet Brianna

Brianna, 24, is in week three of a culinary job training program for women.

  • Scenario 1: the chef criticizes her in front of everyone
  • Scenario 2: she gets a text from her ex during class
  • Scenario 3: she's told she'll lose her stipend for being late
  • A different person de-escalates each time

What Brianna says

“Everybody is always telling me to calm down. Nobody asks why I'm upset.”
Module 05 · Crisis Prevention14
Brianna's quote is the lesson. In each scenario, the facilitator should find out why before trying to fix anything.

Strong teams suggest program changes: private feedback from the chef, a lateness policy for medical visits, and a safety plan with the shelter.

Debrief · whole class 35 min

Step out of role

  1. What did it feel like to play Brianna?
  2. Which scenario was hardest to de-escalate? Why?
  3. What warning signs did you notice early?
  4. What would positive behavior support look like for Brianna next week?
