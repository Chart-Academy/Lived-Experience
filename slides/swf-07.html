Health and work

Health barriers women face

  • Chronic conditions without regular care: diabetes, high blood pressure, asthma
  • Pregnancy, postpartum recovery, and breastfeeding
  • Depression, anxiety, and PTSD
  • Injuries from violence, including head injuries
  • Substance use, and recovery
  • No insurance, no doctor, or no time to go
Module 07 · Health & Wellness6
Ask: “Which of these have you seen keep someone from working, or keeping a job?”

Violence and health

The health effects of violence

  • Head injuries from being hit or strangled can cause headaches, memory problems, and trouble focusing
  • Strangulation can cause serious injury even without visible marks
  • Chronic pain, sleep problems, and stress-related illness
  • Reproductive coercion: an abuser controlling birth control or pregnancy

Know your lane

Don't diagnose. If she describes these, encourage a medical visit and offer a warm handoff.
Module 07 · Health & Wellness7
Many survivors don't connect symptoms like memory problems to past injuries. Helping her get evaluated can change everything, including how she does at work.

Routines

Wellness routines that help keep a job

  • Sleep: a regular bedtime, as much as life allows
  • Food: plan meals and snacks for long shifts
  • Movement: a short walk counts
  • Connection: one person she can call
  • Calm: breathing, prayer, music, or quiet time
  • Appointments: scheduled before she starts work

Her routine

Ask what already works for her. Build on it. Small and realistic beats perfect.

Quick check 10 min

What would you do?

  1. A participant says she's had headaches and trouble remembering things since her ex choked her.
  2. A new mother is worried she can't pump at her new job.
  3. A participant says she's been feeling hopeless and doesn't see the point anymore.
  4. A woman has no health insurance and needs her blood pressure medicine.
Module 07 · Health & Wellness11
1) Encourage an urgent medical visit; strangulation and head injuries need evaluation. 2) Share that she likely has a right to break time and a private space; connect her to HR or an advocate. 3) Ask directly about safety, stay with her, call or text 988, and get your supervisor. 4) Help her apply for coverage and connect to a community health center.

The case · fictional

Meet Patrice

Patrice, 26, had a baby four months ago. She starts a pharmacy technician trainee job in two weeks.

  • Left an abusive relationship while pregnant
  • Breastfeeding her son, Malik
  • Hasn't had a postpartum checkup
  • Excited, and exhausted

What Patrice says

“I want this job so bad. But some days I can barely get out of bed, and I don't know if that's normal.”
Module 07 · Health & Wellness13
Patrice's words may point to postpartum depression, which is common and treatable. The team's job is to encourage care and connect her, not diagnose.
