Module 01 · The Practitioner Role5

SOC 21-1093 · Social and Human Service Assistants

What the job is

  • Meet with clients to find out what they need
  • Help clients find and apply for benefits and services
  • Connect clients to other agencies and follow up
  • Help with day-to-day barriers: forms, ID, transportation
  • Check in on progress and tell the team what changed
  • Keep accurate, private records

You often work under a social worker, counselor, or case manager.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role9
Stress that roles can overlap in real agencies, and that many practitioners started as peers. The point is not which one is better. It is knowing when to refer. Ask: "A client tells you they think they have PTSD. What do you do?" (Listen, don't diagnose, and refer to a clinician with a warm handoff.)

Lived experience is expertise

What you bring that a textbook can't teach

Knowledge

You know how the systems feel from the inside: the waiting room, the forms, the runaround.

Credibility

Clients may trust you faster because you've been there.

Skills you already used

Persistence, problem-solving, reading people, staying calm under pressure.

A responsibility

Your story is yours. Share it on purpose, briefly, and only when it helps the client.

Practice

Read real job postings, then map the systems around a client.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role12

Activity 1 · in pairs 40 min

What do employers actually ask for?

  1. Find three job postings for roles like community liaison, case aide, resource navigator, or outreach worker on Indeed or DC Government Careers.
  2. Highlight every duty in each posting.
  3. On your worksheet (Part A), mark which duties show up in two or three postings.
  4. Be ready to share the top three repeated duties.

No internet? Use the three sample postings on the back of your worksheet.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role13
Circulate. Watch for scam postings (no company name, asks for payment, pay that's too high). Flag them and connect to the Job Search sessions. Debrief: list repeated duties on the board and compare to slide 6.

Activity 2 · small groups 45 min

Build a systems map for Denise

Denise, 34 (fictional), is leaving a shelter with her two children. She was released from jail last year, lost her job when the store closed, and her SNAP is about to expire. Her youngest has asthma.

  1. Put Denise in the center of the map in Part B.
  2. Add each system she touches, and her needs in each one.
  3. Draw lines where one system affects another.
  4. Circle the one place you would start, and say why.
Module 01 · The Practitioner Role14
There is no single right starting point. Good answers explain the reason, for example: "Housing first, because nothing else holds without a stable address," or "SNAP first, because it's about to lapse and it's quick." Groups present in 2 minutes each.
