Part 1

Learn

What the job is, where it happens, and where its limits are.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role5

SOC 21-1093 · Social and Human Service Assistants

What the job is

  • Meet with clients to find out what they need
  • Help clients find and apply for benefits and services
  • Connect clients to other agencies and follow up
  • Help with day-to-day barriers: forms, ID, transportation
  • Check in on progress and tell the team what changed
  • Keep accurate, private records

You often work under a social worker, counselor, or case manager.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role9
Stress that roles can overlap in real agencies, and that many practitioners started as peers. The point is not which one is better. It is knowing when to refer. Ask: "A client tells you they think they have PTSD. What do you do?" (Listen, don't diagnose, and refer to a clinician with a warm handoff.)

Lived experience is expertise

What you bring that a textbook can't teach

Knowledge

You know how the systems feel from the inside: the waiting room, the forms, the runaround.

Credibility

Clients may trust you faster because you've been there.

Skills you already used

Persistence, problem-solving, reading people, staying calm under pressure.

A responsibility

Your story is yours. Share it on purpose, briefly, and only when it helps the client.

Part 2

Practice

Step into a real practitioner role and work one case as a team.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role12
This activity connects today's content to real work. Each person plays a practitioner from a different agency. Everyone sees Denise from a different seat, and no one has the whole picture until the team puts it together. Have the role cards from the in-class packet printed and cut apart, one set per group.

The case · fictional

Meet Denise

Denise, 34, is staying in a family shelter with her two children, ages 4 and 9.

  • She came home from jail 10 months ago.
  • She lost her retail job when the store closed.
  • Her SNAP benefits are about to run out.
  • Her youngest child has asthma.

What she says

“Every office wants something from me before they'll help. I just need one person to help me figure out where to start.”
Module 01 · The Practitioner Role13
Read Denise's quote aloud. Ask: "Has anyone ever felt this way?" Don't push for stories. Just let the room notice how familiar it is.

Module 01 · The Practitioner Role14
Hand out role cards. Let people choose, but encourage them to pick a system they know less about. If a group has fewer than six people, drop the workforce specialist and give that card to the team lead to read aloud during the huddle.

Step 1 · on your own, in role 15 min

See Denise from your seat

  1. Read your role card. Only you know these facts.
  2. Fill in your box on the team map: Denise's needs in your system, and what your agency can offer.
  3. Write down the one thing you need from another agency before you can help her.
Stay in your lane. A practitioner coordinates and connects. You don't diagnose, give legal advice, or promise things your agency can't deliver.
Module 01 · The Practitioner Role15
Circulate and ask each person: "What does your agency do? What can't it do?" This reinforces slide 9 on roles and limits.

Step 2 · team huddle 30 min

Build the systems map together

  1. Go around the table. Each role shares what they know, in role: “At the shelter, I'm seeing…”
  2. The team lead adds each piece to the map, with Denise in the center.
  3. Draw a line wherever one system depends on or blocks another.
  4. Circle the places where Denise is stuck between two systems.
Module 01 · The Practitioner Role16
The facts on the cards are built to collide. For example, she needs an ID for the job and for benefits, income for housing, and her supervision check-ins conflict with the training schedule. If a group is stuck, ask: "Who needs something that someone else at this table controls?"

Step 3 · plan and present 20 min

Where does Denise start?

As a team, decide

  • The first step, and why it comes first
  • The lead practitioner who will be Denise's main contact
  • One warm handoff: who calls whom, and what they say

Present in 2 minutes

Show your map. Share your first step, your lead, and your handoff. Each person says one sentence in role.
Module 01 · The Practitioner Role17
There is no single right answer. Strong teams explain their reasoning and choose one lead, so Denise isn't telling her story six times. Name the idea of a warm handoff now. Module 6 covers it in depth.

Debrief · whole class 15 min

Step out of role

  1. What did your role know that nobody else at the table did?
  2. Where did two systems block each other?
  3. How did it feel to sit on the other side of the desk?
  4. Which of these six jobs could you see yourself doing?
Module 01 · The Practitioner Role18
Question 3 is the bridge to lived experience. Many learners have been the "Denise" in a room like this. Honor that, and point out that this knowledge is exactly what makes them effective practitioners. Question 4 connects to the career ladder without turning class into job search.
