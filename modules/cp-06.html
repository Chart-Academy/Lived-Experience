Resource Navigation in DC

How to find DC resources, check that the information is still right, and connect clients with a warm handoff instead of a phone number on a sticky note. You'll verify real resources as a team and plan a returning citizen's first two weeks.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Locate key DC resources for housing, food, benefits, health, reentry, and employment.
  2. Check eligibility and verify that resource information is current.
  3. Make a warm handoff instead of a cold referral.
  4. Plan around common barriers: ID, transportation, documents, and wait lists.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: Kevin's first two weeks

Kevin Brooks, 26 (fictional), came home from prison two weeks ago. He is sleeping on a friend's couch, has no ID, no income, and an old phone that only works on Wi-Fi. He wants to work and wants to see his daughter.

Each person on your team plays a navigator holding a listing from an old resource guide. Your job is to check whether it is still right, using the official website and a phone call if you can. Then your team plans Kevin's first two weeks, in the right order, with a warm handoff.

Plans the order and the handoff

  1. Verify, in role (20 min): check your card's old listing against the official website, and by phone if you can. Note what changed.
  2. Team huddle (50 min): report back, fill in the verification log, and add your rows to the class resource guide.
  3. Plan and present (40 min): choose Kevin's first three stops in order, plan around his barriers, and role-play one warm handoff call.
  4. Debrief (30 min): step out of role and talk about what you found and what it would have cost Kevin.
