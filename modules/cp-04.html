Module 04 · 6 hours · Learn → Practice → Apply

Communication & Interviewing Skills

The listening and interviewing skills practitioners use every day. You'll learn active listening, the basics of motivational interviewing, and de-escalation, then practice them in rotating triads with three clients who will only open up if you ask the right way.

By the end of this module

Learning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Use active listening skills: open questions, reflecting, and summarizing.
  2. Apply basic motivational interviewing (MI) skills, including OARS and change talk.
  3. Adapt communication for culture, language, literacy, age, and disability.
  4. Use de-escalation techniques with a client who is upset or angry.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6

Triads: three clients, three rounds

Three clients (all fictional) are waiting at a neighborhood service center: Mr. Curtis Bell, 71, a veteran who is hard of hearing; Ms. Ana Morales, 44, who is more comfortable in Spanish; and Mr. Darnell Price, 35, who is angry because his benefits were cut.

Your team splits into triads: practitioner, client, and observer. Each client card has facts the client shares only if the practitioner listens well. You'll rotate three times, so everyone plays every part.

Timekeeper for both triads

  1. Read your cards (20 min): read the client cards, the practitioner guide, and the observer checklist.
  2. Three rounds (50 min): interview for 8 minutes, give feedback for 5, then rotate. Everyone plays practitioner, client, and observer.
  3. Plan and present (40 min): share the questions that opened up each client and the de-escalation words that worked.
  4. Debrief (30 min): step out of role and talk about what it was like to be listened to, or not.

Apply · 20 points · completed and submitted in class

Module 4 assignment

Capture what you learned as practitioner, client, and observer, then reflect on how you listen. You'll complete it in the last part of class and upload it before you leave. There is no homework.

Part 1

Your rounds

What happened when you were the practitioner, and the feedback you received.

Part 2

Your team's question bank

The questions that opened each client up, de-escalation words, and adapting for each person.

Part 3

How I listen

What you learned about yourself as a listener, in your own words.
