Module 03 · 6 hours · Learn → Practice → Apply

Trauma-Informed Practice

How trauma shapes the way people show up for help, and how to make every step of the process safer. You'll learn SAMHSA's six principles, rewrite a harmful intake as a team, and make a plan to care for yourself in this work.

Includes 40 minutes of breaks. There is no homework: everything is submitted by the end of class. Practice cases are fictional. Never bring real client information to class.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Explain the four Rs and the six principles of a trauma-informed approach from SAMHSA.
  2. Recognize common trauma responses and avoid practices that re-traumatize clients.
  3. Rewrite intake questions and everyday language to be trauma-informed.
  4. Create a personal plan to prevent burnout and manage vicarious trauma.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: redesign Rosa's intake

Rosa, 37 (fictional), left an abusive partner three weeks ago with her 12-year-old son. She came to a family services center for help, sat through the center's standard intake, and left halfway through.

Each person on your team plays a staff member who saw part of what happened. Your role card has facts only you know. Together, your team rewrites the intake form so the next person in Rosa's situation feels safe enough to stay.

  1. In role, on your own (20 min): read your card and mark the old intake questions you think caused harm.
  2. Team huddle (50 min): share what you know, then go through each old question: which principle does it break, and how would you rewrite it?
  3. Plan and present (40 min): write the new first five minutes of intake and three changes to the room or process.
  4. Debrief (30 min): step out of role and talk about what it was like on the other side of the desk.

Apply · 20 points · completed and submitted in classModule 3 assignment

Capture the trauma-informed changes your team made to Rosa's intake, then make your own plan for burnout and vicarious trauma. You'll complete it in the last part of class and upload it before you leave. There is no homework.

Part 1

Your role's view

What your role saw, the trauma response you noticed, and the change you'd make.

Part 2

Your team's new intake

Three rewritten questions, the new first five minutes, and the changes to the room.

Part 3

My wellness plan

Your warning signs and your plan for burnout and vicarious trauma, in your own words.
