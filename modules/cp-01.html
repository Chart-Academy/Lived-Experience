Module 01 · 4 hours · Learn → Practice → Apply

The Human Services Practitioner Role

What the job is, where it happens, and where your lived experience fits. You'll learn how the role works, step into it with your team on a real-world case, and connect it to the strengths you already bring.

How the four hours flow

Open · 20 minWelcome and opening circle
Learn · 70 minThe role, settings, DC systems, and role limits
Practice · 90 minTeam roles: build Denise's systems map
Apply · 45 minComplete and submit your assignment, then an exit ticket

Includes a 15-minute break. There is no homework: everything is submitted by the end of class. Practice cases are fictional. Never bring real client information to class.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Describe the duties of a Social and Human Service Assistant (SOC 21-1093) and three settings where they work.
  2. Map the main DC service systems a client may touch: housing, benefits, health, justice, and employment.
  3. Explain the difference between peer support, practitioner, and clinical roles, and the limits of each.
  4. Name three strengths from their own lived experience that apply to this work.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: Denise's systems map

Denise, 34 (fictional), is staying in a family shelter with her two children. She came home from jail 10 months ago, lost her job when the store closed, and her SNAP benefits are about to run out. Her youngest child has asthma.

Each person on your team plays a practitioner from a different agency. Your role card has facts about Denise that only you know. No one can see the whole picture until the team puts it together.

Runs the meeting, keeps time, draws the map

  1. In role, on your own (15 min): read your card and fill in your part of the team map.
  2. Team huddle (30 min): share what you know, draw where systems depend on or block each other.
  3. Plan and present (20 min): choose Denise's first step, her lead practitioner, and one warm handoff.
  4. Debrief (15 min): step out of role and talk about what it was like on the other side of the desk.

Apply · 20 points · completed and submitted in classModule 1 assignment

Capture what you learned as a practitioner on Denise's team, then connect it to your own life. You'll complete it in the last part of class and upload it before you leave. There is no homework.

Part 1

Your role on the team

What your role knew, what it needed from other agencies, and one limit of the role.

Part 2

Your team's systems map

Denise's needs in each system, where systems collide, your first step, and a warm handoff.

Part 3

Lived experience reflection

Three strengths from your own life and a short reflection, written in your own words.
