Person-Centered Practices & Mobility Planning

How to lead a conversation that surfaces a woman's own goals, strengths, and preferences, and turn it into a workforce readiness and economic mobility plan. You'll practice the conversation and write the plan as a team.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Lead a person-centered conversation that surfaces a participant's goals, strengths, and preferences.
  2. Write a workforce readiness and economic mobility plan with short- and long-term steps, including child care and rebuilding credit.
  3. Support choice and self-determination, even when you disagree.
  4. Document evidence of person-centered practice for a Core Competency E-Badge.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: Tasha's mobility plan

Tasha Greene, 39 (fictional), came home three months ago after six years in federal prison. Her 15-year-old daughter lives with Tasha's mother. Tasha is staying in a women's reentry house and has a part-time job at a warehouse.

One person plays Tasha, and her card has goals she shares only if asked the right way. The facilitator leads a goal conversation while the team listens. Then the team writes Tasha's mobility plan together, with each role adding what it knows.

  1. Read your card (20 min): get ready for the conversation. Tasha and the facilitator prepare. Everyone else reads what they know.
  2. Goal conversation and huddle (50 min): the facilitator and Tasha talk for 10 minutes while the team listens. Then the team plans together.
  3. Plan and present (40 min): write Tasha's mobility plan, check it with Tasha, and present.
  4. Debrief (30 min): step out of role and talk about what it's like to be planned for, or planned with.
