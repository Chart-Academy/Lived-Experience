Module 02 · 5 hours · Learn → Practice → Apply

Ethics, Boundaries & Confidentiality

The ethical standards of human services, what you can and can't keep private, and how to hold boundaries when you work in your own community. You'll learn the standards, work through real dilemmas as a team, and plan for the boundaries you'll need to watch.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Apply the National Organization for Human Services (NOHS) Ethical Standards for Human Services Professionals to practice situations.
  2. Explain confidentiality, informed consent, and the limits of confidentiality, including mandated reporting.
  3. Recognize boundary risks and dual relationships when working in one's own community, and plan how to handle them.
  4. Identify information that may never be shared without consent, and how to say no respectfully.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: Tanya's ethics case conference

Tanya, 29 (fictional), is a single mother of a 6-year-old who has come to a neighborhood resource center for help with back rent. The center is in the same neighborhood where one of its outreach workers grew up, and several ethical questions come up in the same week.

Each person on your team plays a staff member who faced one of those questions. Your role card has the facts only you know. In the case conference, your team uses the six steps to decide what to do about each one.

Runs the case conference

  1. In role, on your own (20 min): read your card and decide what you think is right before you hear the others.
  2. Team huddle (40 min): each role presents its question. Use the six steps and fill in the decision table.
  3. Plan and present (30 min): pick the hardest question, explain your decision, and write what you'll say to Tanya.
  4. Debrief (25 min): step out of role and talk about what it was like on the other side of the desk.

Apply · 20 points · completed and submitted in classModule 2 assignment

Capture the ethical thinking from your team's case conference, then plan for the boundaries in your own community. You'll complete it in the last part of class and upload it before you leave. There is no homework.

Part 1

Your role's question

The dilemma your role faced, the standard that applies, and what you'd say.

Part 2

Your team's decisions

Your team's calls on all six questions, mandated reporting steps, and the limits of confidentiality in plain words.

Part 3

Boundaries in my community

The boundary risks in your own community and your plan to handle them, in your own words.
