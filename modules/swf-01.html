The Facilitator Role & NADSP Code of Ethics

What a survivor workforce facilitator does, the NADSP Code of Ethics that guides direct support, and how to hold boundaries when a woman's story mirrors your own. You'll work through real ethical situations as a team and draft the reflection for your first E-Badge.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Describe the duties of a survivor workforce facilitator and a direct support professional in programs that serve women.
  2. Explain each tenet of the NADSP Code of Ethics with a workplace example.
  3. Set healthy boundaries when supporting women whose experiences may mirror their own.
  4. Prepare a written reflection for the Code of Ethics Commitment E-Badge.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: ethics at Women Rising

Angela, 31 (fictional), joined Women Rising, a workforce program for women survivors, after leaving an abusive relationship and spending 18 months in a shelter. She wants a full-time job with benefits. This month, several staff members each hit an ethical question about her case.

Each person on your team plays a staff member at Women Rising. Your role card has facts only you know. Together, your team decides what to do about each question, using the NADSP Code of Ethics.

Runs the meeting

  1. In role, on your own (20 min): read your card and decide which tenet applies before you hear the others.
  2. Team huddle (40 min): each role presents its question. Name the tenet, list the options, and decide together.
  3. Plan and present (30 min): choose the hardest question, explain your decision, and write what you'll say to Angela.
  4. Debrief (25 min): step out of role and talk about what it was like on the other side of the desk.
