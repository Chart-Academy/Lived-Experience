Module 07 · 5 hours · Learn → Practice → Apply

Documentation & Professional Writing

How to write case notes and professional messages that are accurate, respectful, and private. You'll learn the DAP format, objective and strengths-based language, and the basic privacy rules, then fix a set of weak notes as a team.

By the end of this moduleLearning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Write case notes in the DAP (Data, Assessment, Plan) format.
  2. Use objective, strengths-based language instead of judgments or labels.
  3. Store and share records according to privacy rules, including HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2.
  4. Proofread for a professional tone in notes and emails.

Practice · in class · groups of 4–6Team roles: fix the notes from Ms. Hayes's visit

Ms. Lorraine Hayes, 59 (fictional), had a home visit from a team of workers after she fell behind on her utility bill and stopped picking up her medications. Every worker wrote a note about the same visit, and none of the notes are good enough.

Each person on your team plays one of the workers. Your card has your weak note and what really happened. Together, your team finds the problems, rewrites one strong DAP note for the visit, and writes a referral email.

  1. In role, on your own (20 min): read your weak note and what really happened. List every problem you can find.
  2. Team huddle (40 min): run the four proofreading checks on each note, then sort the facts into Data, Assessment, and Plan.
  3. Plan and present (30 min): write one strong DAP note for the visit and a referral email, then present.
  4. Debrief (25 min): step out of role and talk about what it's like to read a note about yourself.

How it's graded

Your note · 5Three real problems matched to the right check, and a rewrite that passes all four.
DAP note · 5Facts in Data, a fair Assessment based on them, and a Plan with who, what, and when.
Email and privacy · 5A clear, professional email with minimum necessary detail, and correct privacy reasoning.
Reflection · 5Thoughtful, specific, and in the learner's own words.
