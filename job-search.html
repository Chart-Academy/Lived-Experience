J2Résumé from Lived Experience
Translate what you've done into language employers hire for.3 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Turn work, volunteer, peer, caregiving, and lived experience into action-and-result bullet points.
  2. Add the course credential and competencies to a résumé accurately.
  3. Explain a gap or justice involvement in one honest, forward-looking sentence.
  4. Tailor a résumé to a real posting without adding anything that isn't true.

In-class activity

  1. Remove your address, phone, and email, then ask AI to review your résumé against a checklist.
  2. Make the three changes that matter most yourself, then tailor a copy to your target posting.
AI Instructions: Only suggest rewrites based on experience the student actually has. Never invent jobs, dates, credentials, or skills. Ask the student to explain each change in their own words.

J3Finding Openings & Avoiding Scams
Real jobs that fit you, and the red flags of fake ones.3 hrs

Learning objectives · the learner will be able to:

  1. Search Indeed, DC Government Careers, and nonprofit job boards using filters and job alerts.
  2. Use a Skills-to-Openings agent to compare 10 real openings against their must-haves.
  3. Identify red flags of a scam listing, such as upfront fees or requests for bank details.

In-class activity

  1. Rank 10 real openings and mark each as good fit, not a fit, or scam.
AI Instructions: Never log in to a job site or apply for the student. Do not include listings that ask for upfront fees or personal financial information.

Ground rules for job searching with AI

  • Remove your address, phone, and email before sharing your résumé with AI.
  • Every line on your résumé must describe something you actually did.
  • Search and apply for jobs yourself. Never let an AI tool log in to a job site or apply for you.
  • Never pay a fee to get a job, and never share bank details or your Social Security number before a verified offer.
