Both courses · 20 hours · plan, implement, present

Capstone

Your capstone is where you plan and carry out the strategies you learned. You'll work in small teams on a real community need, using fictional or fully de-identified cases, and finish by presenting what you did and what you learned.

Four phases

How the 20 hours work

  1. 1. Plan · 4 hrsChoose a community need, write a goal, and map the strategies you'll use.
  2. 2. Build · 6 hrsCreate the tools: guides, plans, trackers, session materials.
  3. 3. Implement · 6 hrsCarry out the plan through role-plays, field practice, or a live session.
  4. 4. Present + Reflect · 4 hrsPresent results to peers and employer partners, then reflect.
Certified Community Practitioner

Community Resource Navigation Project

Choose one DC ward and one population (for example, returning citizens, young parents, or older adults). Build and test a navigation strategy that helps that population reach the services it needs.

  1. Plan. Name the population and its top three needs. Write one SMART goal for your project.
  2. Build. Create a verified resource guide with at least 15 resources, an intake form that is trauma-informed, and a referral tracker in Google Sheets.
  3. Implement. Work a fictional case from intake to service plan to warm handoff, with written case notes. Role-play it for your instructor.
  4. Present. Share your guide, case, and lessons learned in a 10-minute presentation.
In your own words · write this part yourself
  • “The need I focused on was … because …”
  • “The strategy that worked best was … and I'd change … next time.”
  • “This project prepared me for the HS-BCP and my target job by …”

Certified Survivor Workforce Facilitator

Women Survivors' Workforce Readiness Workshop

Design and co-facilitate a workforce readiness session for women survivors, then support one fictional woman from stabilization to a job-ready plan.

  1. Plan. Choose a session topic (for example, explaining a gap, workplace safety, or first-week success). Write learning goals and group agreements.
  2. Build. Create a facilitator guide, a participant handout, a safety plan template, and a referral list.
  3. Implement. Co-facilitate a 45-minute session with your cohort. Then complete a person-centered mobility plan and progress note for one fictional participant.
  4. Present. Share your session materials, peer feedback, and what you'd improve. Organize your evidence for your NADSP E-Badges.
In your own words · write this part yourself
  • “I made the session feel safe by …”
  • “A moment I had to adjust during facilitation was … and I …”
  • “This project gave me evidence for these DSP-I badges: …”

Not yet (2): No session evidence and no participant plan.

  • Your case notes or session feedback
  • Your presentation slides
  • Your own-words reflection
